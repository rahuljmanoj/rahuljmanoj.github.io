<p>
Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the world’s leading cause of death, claiming
<strong>~19&nbsp;million lives every year</strong>.  A striking share of these fatalities are
<em>premature</em>—people who die between 40 and 60 years of age.  Around 40 % of all
CVD cases eventually progress to heart failure (HF), which comes in two flavours:
<ul>
  <li><strong>HFrEF</strong>—heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (a pumping problem);</li>
  <li><strong>HFpEF</strong>—heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (a filling problem).</li>
</ul>
</p>

<p>
HFrEF is relatively easy to spot on a standard echocardiogram because the left-ventricular ejection fraction (EF) is visibly reduced.  HFpEF is a tougher nut to crack: conventional echo-based biomarkers are <strong>inconclusive in 30 – 40 % of cases</strong>.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>Why does HFpEF slip through the net?</h2>

<p>
The key pathophysiological clue is an elevated <strong>left-ventricular filling pressure&nbsp;(LVFP)</strong>, often expressed as the end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP).  The gold-standard way to measure LVFP is invasive cardiac catheterisation—an option no clinician relishes for routine HFpEF screening because it is costly, uncomfortable, and resource-intensive.
</p>

<p>
A <em>non-invasive</em> LVFP metric is therefore an urgent, unmet clinical need.
</p>

</p>

<hr>

<h2>ECHO-PULSE-TWIN — our solution</h2>

<p>
Our team’s solution—<strong>ECHO-PULSE-TWIN</strong>—builds a <em>digital twin</em> of a patient’s cardiovascular system by fusing three routine data streams:
</p>

<ol>
  <li>Standard cardiac echocardiography,</li>
  <li>Carotid duplex ultrasound scans,</li>
  <li>Brachial blood-pressure readings, and</li>
  <li>Shear-wave elastography of the left-ventricular wall.</li>
</ol>

<p>
Everything runs on <em>existing</em> ultrasound hardware; no additional probes or catheters are required.  The model translates your everyday echo scan into a real-time functional report, enabling clinicians to detect diastolic dysfunction earlier and with far greater confidence.
</p>
